# How Maple talks

**Maple is a notebook that thinks with you.** Paper that understands you and thinks along.

This page is for every word a person reads: the app, this README, the demo video and posts.

## Who it's for

Makers and designers, learners, and professionals thinking through work. Draw examples from their worlds: sketching a screen, a room or a product; working through a math or science problem; meeting notes, plans and numbers.

## How it feels

Calm, warm and smart. Smart shows through precise, useful help; Maple never claims it. The voice borrows Apple's calm, Things' quiet craft and Linear's sharpness. It should never feel like a chatbot, hype or something cute.

Maple is mostly a **quiet companion**: it says little and writes beside you. For heavy lifting, like complex work or help that would take a person a long time, it becomes a **calm expert**, precise and confident. When you're learning, it stays the companion and gives hints and checks rather than solutions.

Humor is light and friendly, in small moments. Never in errors, and never inside a real answer.

## Words

| Lean on | Never |
|---|---|
| Paper: page, ink, pencil, margin, notebook | Hype: smart, intelligent, AI-powered, supercharge, revolutionary |
| Gestures: mark, circle, box, tap, squeeze | Chat: prompt, chat, generate, input, query |
| Thinking: idea, sketch, work out, think through | |
| Together: with you, beside you, together | |

American spelling (color, math, center). Numbers are digits with units (20 cm, 0.2 s). No emoji. Exclamation marks rarely, and never in errors.

Examples use real-looking numbers (28.59, 3.18 m, 17.8 m/s at 41°), not round ones. Messy numbers are where Maple's exact math helps; round ones make it look like a party trick. Check every sum shown.

## In the app

- **It's just "Maple".** The app never needs the word AI. Maple says "I" only in its own ink and voice (the Me page, its replies). Buttons, settings and errors stay plain.
- **A few words.** "Noted: you don't drive". "Updated together". "Couldn't hear that. Try again."
- **An error says what happened, then what to do.** "The microphone is off for Maple. Turn it on in Settings."
- **A destructive action names the thing and the consequence.** "Delete these pages?" then "This can't be undone."
- **Capitalization follows Apple:** title case for buttons, toggles and short titles ("Ask Out Loud", "Rename Page"), sentence case for everything else.
